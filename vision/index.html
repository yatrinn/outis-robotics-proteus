Outis / Our vision

Intelligence should be able to choose its body.

Smaller modules. More possible machines.
Thousands of modules that become whatever the moment needs.

Yannik Trinn & Ruben Estrella · 24 October 2026 · 3 min read

The idea

Where this goes Why small matters How we get there Embodied AI The plan

Computers became universal when one machine could run any program. Robots have not had that moment yet. Each one is still built around a single body, and that body decides where it can go, what it can reach and how hard it can push.

Robot brains are getting better every month. The body has become the bottleneck.

Proteus makes the body programmable.

Where this goes

Picture a pool of thousands of small modules. Give it a task, and it takes the shape that task needs. Give it two, and it splits into two machines that work side by side, then merges back into one when the job needs strength. The body is never fixed. It is always the right one for the moment.

A humanoid has one body. A pool of modules has as many as the work requires, on a factory floor, in a home or on a space station. We think that pool will one day be as ordinary as a toolbox.

That is the embodied AI we are building: one that learns not only what to do, but which body, and how many bodies, to do it with.

Why small matters

Everything depends on the size of the module. Halve it, and the same space holds eight times as many: finer structures, more shapes, more machines at once. Small and numerous, modules stop behaving like one robot and start working like a team.

Each step down takes new actuators, docking mechanisms and manufacturing methods. That is the deep-tech core of our company, and the reason we build our hardware ourselves.

How we get there

Every generation goes to work as soon as it can do real work. Today’s modules are sized for industrial equipment, so we start where they already make a difference: inspection, maintenance and rescue, where people still have to climb into vessels, tanks and voids.

Our design partners will get a robot that goes in first. We will get field tests, revenue and real-world data no lab can produce. Each generation is smaller and more capable than the last, and each one opens new kinds of work.

Embodied AI that chooses its body

Ask for a task, “place this sensor behind that opening”, and the system decides which machines to become, how many, how to assemble them and how to move them. Our planner already works out structures and docking sequences on the real geometry of our modules.

Every deployment teaches it which shapes work and how to recover when they don’t. The big labs are building better brains, and we will use the best of them. We build the bodies they can change, and the real-world data that comes with them. That data compounds.

The plan

  1. Build a module that docks, carries load and moves under its own power.
  2. Take it into the field where people still have to climb in.Inspection, maintenance and rescue, with design partners.
  3. Shrink it, generation by generation.Each size opens new work, and that work funds the next one.
  4. Let intelligence choose the body, and how many bodies.One platform for physical work, from industry to everyday life and beyond.

A task should be able to call forth the machine it needs. We are building that future in Berlin, one module at a time.
