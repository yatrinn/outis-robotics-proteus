Outis / Our vision

Intelligence should be able to choose its body.

Smaller modules. More possible machines.
A pool of modules that becomes the robot the job needs.

Yannik Trinn & Ruben Estrella · 24 October 2026 · 3 min read

The idea

Any body the job needs Smaller modules Embodied AI The plan Where we are

Robot brains are improving fast. But a robot can only work where its body fits, and only push as hard as its body can brace. The places where work is hardest, inside a vessel, behind a manhole, under rubble, were not built for robots, and they were not built for humans either.

Humanoids need a world built for humans. Proteus adapts its body to the world.

One pool of modules. Any body the job needs.

Proteus is built from one part: a powered strut with a docking head at each end. The struts connect, carry load and release, so one kit is designed to enter a vessel as a slim chain, brace itself into a stiff truss around a tool, or walk over rubble. On the next job, it becomes a different machine.

The bigger the pool and the smaller the parts, the more machines it can become. That is the whole idea: a task should be able to call forth the machine it needs.

Think smaller. Build more.

Today our MK1 module is about 11 cm across and 45 cm long, the size of a forearm. Our roadmap halves it with every generation. Each halving fits eight times as many modules into the same space: they pass narrower openings, form finer structures and take on more shapes. Three halvings take the module to roughly the thickness of a marker pen, and the same volume then holds 512 times as many.

We know where this gets hard. The first two halvings are engineering. Beyond them, friction, tolerances and connectors start to dominate, and the module needs new kinds of actuators. That part is research, and we treat it as research. Each size has to earn its place with a job that pays for it.

Embodied AI that chooses its body.

A command like “place this sensor behind that opening” hides several problems: understand the space, pick the tool, choose a structure, find a safe sequence to build it, then control the machine that results. Our planner already finds docking sequences on our module’s real kinematics; forces and dynamics come next.

Every deployment then teaches the system which shapes work, where transitions fail and how to recover, with real-world data no simulation can replace. The big labs will keep building better brains, and we will use the best ones. We build the body they can change, and collect the data from the places where it works. That data is what compounds.

The plan

  1. Build one module that docks, carries load and moves under its own power.Done: MK1
  2. Do paid work where a person still has to climb in.Inspection and handling inside vessels and machinery: enter as a chain, brace into a truss, hold the tool steady.Next
  3. Halve the module with every generation.Each size opens new jobs, and each job pays for the next size.
  4. Let intelligence choose the body.A pool of modules and a planner that picks the structure, builds it and learns from every deployment.

Where we are

We have built MK1 in Berlin since mid-September. Its heads move under their own power, its docks carry load, and simple powered docking works on our test rig. We have filed a German utility-model application with 34 claims. Not done yet: docking under load and a braced structure of several modules. Those come next.

Bring us a job where a person still has to climb in. We will show you what Proteus can do there, and where it can’t yet.
