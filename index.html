The problem

Someone still has to go in first.

Inspection and rescue often mean entering unstable structures, crossing rubble or reaching into confined spaces. Each step asks something different of a robot’s body.

Send a robot. Lose a module, not a person.

The module

Everything is made of one part.

One actuated strut with our own mechanical docking head at each end. Identical struts lock together, carry load across every joint, share power and data, and change shape while they work. The docking mechanism is IP‑protected.

  1. Load-bearing dockOur IP‑protected mechanical latch locks two modules together and carries load across the joint.
  2. Six degrees of freedomThree linear actuators at each end move each head, so modules can reach, dock and change shape.
  3. One strut, many rolesThe same part works as a leg, a link in a chain or a brace in a truss.
  4. Power through the dockPower and data pass from module to module, so the whole structure acts as one machine.

How it works

Today’s robots are stuck with the body they were built with.

When the task or terrain changes, they fail. Proteus modules dock into whatever body the job needs: a walker over rubble, a chain through narrow openings, a braced arm that works with force on site.

1Walker

Walks where wheels stop.

Crosses rubble, steps and gaps with the sensor or tool on board. Nobody has to cross first.

2Chain

Fits through narrow openings.

Turns into a chain and reaches through manways, nozzles, hatches and gaps in collapsed slabs.

3Braced arm

Works with force, on site.

Braces against the structure and presses tools and sensors on with a force a snake or a drone cannot apply.

Each of today’s robots covers one part of the job. Proteus covers all of it, with one kit.

For example: cross rubble as a walker, slip through a gap as a chain, then brace and hold a sensor against a cracked column. Today that job means a person going in.

What it does

Goes where people shouldn’t have to.

Proteus inspects, retrieves and works with force where access is hard and entry is dangerous.

Inspect

Brings cameras and sensors into hard-to-reach spaces, so conditions are known before anyone enters.

Retrieve and place

Reaches for objects and positions sensors or tools where they are needed.

Work with force

Braces against the structure to press a sensor onto a surface or hold a tool in place.

Our vision

Proteus redefines embodied AI.

Computers became universal when one machine could run any program. Robots have not had that moment yet. Each one is still built around a single body, and that body decides where it can go, what it can reach and how hard it can push. Robot brains get better every month. The body has become the bottleneck.

Proteus makes the body a choice. A pool of identical modules becomes the machine a task needs, splits into a team when there are several tasks and merges back when the job needs strength. One part, made by the million, does the work of a fleet of specialized machines: a broken module is swapped, not the machine, and capacity grows the way compute does.

Modular robots have spent decades in labs, built to prove that reconfiguration works. Ours is built for paid work from the first module on, with load-bearing docks and our own swarm intelligence that decides which body to become and moves the modules as one. Each generation gets smaller and opens new work.
